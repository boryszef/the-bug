Absolutely. With that addition, the setting becomes much more distinctive: **the alien material isn't merely a resource that caused the apocalypse—it is the missing technological link between biology and computation.** The player gradually discovers that.

# Design board — current MVP

## 1. The world

### Time

**~500 years from now.**

The player lives around the year **2526**, give or take.

The important historical milestones are:

```text
~2026  Our present
  │
  │  technological development
  │
~2226  Extraterrestrial materials discovered
  │
  │  "edge computing" problem emerges
  │  computing organisms developed
  │
  │  technology spreads / becomes indispensable
  │
~2326  Ecological catastrophe begins
  │
  │  engineered organisms escape control
  │  ecosystem transforms
  │  human civilization collapses
  │
  │  ~200 years of adaptation
  │
~2526  The game
```

The precise dates aren't important yet; the **~200 years between the invention of computing organisms and the present day** is the useful part.

---

# 2. The technological revolution

Around **300 years after our time**, humanity encounters a new problem.

Traditional computing has become inadequate for certain applications because there are simply too many distributed systems requiring real-time processing.

The solution is **edge computing taken to an extreme**.

Scientists discover that certain extraterrestrial materials possess unusual physical properties that can be exploited for computation.

Large quantities of these materials are brought to Earth.

This enables something previously impossible:

> **Living organisms that can perform computation.**

Rather than putting a computer *inside* an organism, scientists learn to make the organism itself grow computational structures.

The technology is revolutionary.

Plants and simple organisms can now:

* sense their environment,
* process information,
* communicate,
* perform simple computation,
* adapt their behaviour,
* potentially coordinate with other organisms.

Initially, this is completely controlled.

And extremely useful.

---

# 3. The catastrophe

The technology eventually escapes its intended boundaries.

Engineered plants, fungi, insects and other simple organisms begin incorporating the extraterrestrial materials into their biological structures.

The crucial problem isn't necessarily that somebody programmed them to attack humans.

It's that **the organisms can adapt**.

They were designed to solve local problems:

> detect → compute → react.

But now there are millions or billions of them.

And they begin interacting.

The system becomes something its creators no longer understand.

Perhaps individual organisms are not particularly intelligent.

But collectively they produce behaviours that nobody predicted.

The ecosystem starts changing.

Some organisms become:

* extremely aggressive,
* toxic,
* invasive,
* electrically active,
* capable of sensing humans,
* capable of coordinating,
* capable of altering their environment.

The exact mechanism of the catastrophe is deliberately **unknown to the player**.

It's part of the mystery.

---

# 4. The present-day ecosystem

Two centuries after the collapse, the catastrophe has largely **stabilized**.

Earth isn't continuously becoming more dangerous.

Instead, a new ecosystem has emerged.

Humanity has learned to coexist with it.

There are:

### Settlements

Small human communities occupy relatively safe regions.

They have limited technology and rely heavily on:

* scavenging,
* agriculture,
* simple manufacturing,
* salvaged technology.

### Wild regions

Nature has reclaimed most of the planet.

They contain ordinary organisms alongside descendants of the engineered ones.

### Hostile regions

Some areas are essentially inaccessible to ordinary people.

They may contain:

* poisonous vegetation,
* predatory plants,
* aggressive insects,
* electrically active organisms,
* strange fungal ecosystems,
* concentrations of extraterrestrial materials.

People have accumulated a large body of **practical knowledge** about these regions.

But they don't necessarily understand *why* things behave that way.

---

# 5. The ruins

The player explores a world containing **two different archaeological layers**.

### Our civilization

Artifacts from approximately 500 years ago:

* cars
* phones
* computers
* cables
* batteries
* tools
* buildings
* industrial equipment

These objects are sometimes found buried beneath the newer civilization.

To the player, some may be ancient and mysterious.

Others may be surprisingly recognizable.

### The advanced civilization

Artifacts from the period immediately preceding the collapse:

* advanced materials
* autonomous machines
* genetic engineering equipment
* computational devices
* biological technology
* extraterrestrial-material processing systems

These are much harder to understand.

And the two layers can physically overlap.

A player might dig through an abandoned building and find:

> an old smartphone

sitting next to:

> a 300-year-old biological computing component.

That juxtaposition could become one of the game's visual signatures.

---

# 6. The extraterrestrial materials

These are particularly important.

They were brought to Earth in **large quantities**, so they aren't merely lost curiosities.

After the collapse they became incorporated into the ecosystem.

Consequently, the player can encounter them in several forms:

* raw deposits
* fragments of old industrial material
* mineralized biological structures
* plant tissues
* insect structures
* abandoned technological components
* processed materials
* perhaps materials that have been biologically transformed

This gives us an elegant explanation for why the player can find technologically extraordinary materials **in nature**.

And importantly:

> **The player doesn't initially know they're extraterrestrial.**

They're simply strange materials with unusual properties.

---

# 7. The player

The protagonist is deliberately ordinary.

No chosen one.

No superhuman abilities.

No special ancestry.

They're simply:

> **a curious person with a knack for observation, experimentation and making things.**

Perhaps they are already a scavenger, tinkerer, repairer, gatherer, or simply someone who likes investigating old ruins.

Their initial motivation can be very small:

> *"I found something weird. I wonder what it does."*

That gradually becomes:

> *"This material behaves in ways I've never seen."*

Then:

> *"Someone engineered these organisms."*

Then:

> *"They weren't made on Earth."*

And eventually:

> **"I need to find out what happened."**

---

# 8. Core game loop

The game is fundamentally:

**Explore → Gather → Examine → Experiment → Craft → Discover → Explore further**

### Exploration

Visit locations and search for resources.

The player encounters:

* ordinary materials,
* rare resources,
* old artifacts,
* biological specimens,
* unknown materials.

### Gathering

Resources have different rarity and properties.

For example:

```text
Copper wire
  metal
  conductive
  flexible
  manufactured
```

versus:

```text
Unknown crystal
  mineral
  conductive
  photoreactive
  information-bearing
  extraterrestrial?
```

The last property may not initially be known to the player.

---

# 9. Properties are fundamental

This is one of the strongest mechanical ideas so far.

Items aren't merely identified by their names.

They have **properties** that determine what they can be used for.

For example:

```text
Material A
  conductive
  flexible

Material B
  conductive
  crystalline

Material C
  information-bearing
  crystalline
```

This can make crafting substantially more interesting than:

> 3 × Iron → Sword.

Recipes can effectively be **relationships between properties**.

The player gradually learns:

> conductive + crystalline → useful for computation

and later:

> conductive + crystalline + information-bearing → biological computing component

The player is therefore learning the technology while playing.

---

# 10. Recipe discovery

Recipes are **not presented as a complete technology tree**.

The player discovers them.

Sources of discovery could include:

### Experimentation

> "What happens if I combine these?"

### Old artifacts

A damaged document might reveal part of a process.

### Observation

Finding a plant that naturally produces something teaches the player about a material.

### Reverse engineering

Disassembling an old device reveals components.

### Knowledge

Once the player understands certain properties, previously incomprehensible combinations become obvious.

So recipes themselves become part of progression.

---

# 11. Crafting progression

The progression should feel approximately like:

```text
Natural materials
       ↓
Basic processing
       ↓
Tools
       ↓
Refined materials
       ↓
Components
       ↓
Machines
       ↓
Advanced machines
       ↓
Computational technology
       ↓
Biological / extraterrestrial technology
       ↓
The ultimate machine
```

The important thing is that **the player doesn't know the entire tree beforehand**.

They're reconstructing it.

---

# 12. The ultimate goal

The long-term objective is:

> **Build an advanced machine capable of reconstructing enough information about the old world to determine what actually caused the catastrophe.**

The player isn't just building:

> "the biggest computer."

They're assembling something that combines technologies from different eras.

Potentially:

* conventional electronics,
* recovered data storage,
* advanced sensors,
* material analysis,
* biological computation,
* extraterrestrial materials,
* perhaps even living computational components.

The machine represents the culmination of everything the player has learned.

---

# 13. The central mystery

At the beginning, the player knows roughly what everyone else knows:

> **Something happened. The old civilization collapsed. The engineered organisms are dangerous.**

But that's about it.

Over time, they discover:

### Layer 1

The hostile organisms are engineered.

### Layer 2

Their strange computational properties are connected to a material nobody understands.

### Layer 3

The material isn't originally from Earth.

### Layer 4

Huge quantities of it were brought to Earth centuries ago.

### Layer 5

The organisms weren't simply programmed weapons.

### Layer 6

Something about their ability to adapt and interact caused the system to behave unexpectedly.

### Final layer

**What actually happened?**

And importantly, we don't have to decide the final answer yet.

For the MVP, **the existence of this mystery is enough**.

---

# 14. What makes this setting distinctive

I think the combination now has a pretty strong identity:

> **Post-apocalyptic ecological exploration + crafting + archaeology + speculative computing.**

It's not quite:

* Fallout — because the focus isn't conventional survival or warfare.
* Horizon — because the strange organisms aren't simply robotic animals.
* S.T.A.L.K.E.R. — because the mystery isn't primarily about a contaminated zone.
* traditional fantasy — despite having very strange "magical" materials.
* conventional sci-fi — because the technology has become part of nature.

The key visual/conceptual motif could be:

> **Nature has become technological without ceasing to be nature.**

A tree isn't hiding a computer in its trunk.

**The tree itself has become a computer.**

And the player has to figure out how and why.
